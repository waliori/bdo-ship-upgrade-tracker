Bartering, part three. At the wharf: the plan becomes goods in your hold.
Same board, same sailor, sailing from Iliya Island. This time we take the run that makes the most silver.
Click "Load at the wharf".
The hold, as it stands: its weight against the ship’s limit, and its slots. A Carrack Advance has forty.
"Open the hold" lists every good aboard, with what a barterer pays for it, and every good waiting in the storage at your port, ready to load.
Beside it, what there is to spend: the Parley, the vouchers, and what one trade costs at your barter level.
This run is cut into trips. The hold cannot carry every chain’s first goods at once, so the run comes back to Iliya for the rest.
Trip one is loaded now.
Trip two is picked up at the wharf on the way, at stop nine. Each trip can be moved sooner or later with the arrows, or left out.
Buy at the Market: the land goods the chains start from, how many, and what they cost.
Tick a row when it is in your hold in game. The tick is real: the silver comes off your purse, and the goods go into the ship’s hold in the app.
If your own workers make a good, click "my workers", and it costs the run nothing.
Take from storage: goods the run climbs from, waiting at Iliya. "Tick them all" moves them from the storage to the hold.
They show under "Already aboard" now, checked against the hold, each with "put it back".
A tick never loads more than the hull has slots for. Level 5 and up take a slot each; if they do not fit, it loads what fits and says what did not.
In the storage after: what comes home to Iliya, and the silver the wharf calls bring in.
The dailies handed in at Iliya before casting off, and the ones this run passes by.
Then the route, stop by stop: how many stops, how far, how long, and the quests handed in on the way.
"Draw it on the chart" puts the whole route on the Map, in this order.
Chains are climbed together, as many at once as the hold carries, the tops sold before the next lot.
The rations: what the run eats, as an estimate. A tick every seven seconds: the hull’s share, plus the crew’s appetite.
The first card is the hold as the lines are let go.
Every stop says what you hand over, what you get back, and how many times.
And on the right, the hold, its slots, the Parley and the rations after the stop.
Where the Parley would run short, a voucher is drawn on — here, at the first island.
Each leg is drawn on a small chart.
A wharf call: buy supplies here, the goods it loads from storage for the next trip, what it sells and for how much, and the quests handed in.
Every stop can be moved sooner or later, or skipped. Skip one, and the route is laid again round it.
The change can be taken back with Undo, at the top of the page.
One more thing. Back on the plan, choose "The whole board, loaded heavy".
A wharf loads the hold only up to its limit. The exchanges can take it further, up to 170 percent.
An amber figure means the hold is over its limit there. You still sail, only slower, until a wharf takes the extra off.

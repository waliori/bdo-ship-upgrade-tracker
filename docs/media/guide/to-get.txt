This is To Get. Once a build is queued, it answers what is left: what to do next, and when it will be done.
Still to get, at the top: everything you are short of, biggest first, tinted by what the shop would charge for it.
Press one, and the whole screen narrows to that one thing.
Press it again, and everything is back.
The list copies out as text, or as rows for a spreadsheet.
Two readings below it. The way to get it picks one way for each thing. Every way lists them all.
The plan opens on the headline: how many days until you have the lot.
Under it, the thing that sets that pace. Barter days are always called an estimate, since which board a refresh deals is chance.
Beside it, the coins it will spend against what you can spare, and how many things are left, across how many steps.
Then it asks what matters most to you, because there is no right answer.
Soonest spends the purse wherever that buys a day.
Keep the coins spends Crow Coins only where nothing else sells the thing.
Keep the silver leaves the Central Market alone, and buys from Falasi only what only he sells.
The day count and the spend follow every choice. With this purse all three land on the same day, so the goal only moves the plan where there is a real choice to make.
Tell it how many days a week you actually sail. Three days a week, and the same plan takes longer on the calendar.
Type the Crow Coins you want to keep back. The plan never spends them, and works around what is left.
And say what you are willing to do at all.
Switch the dailies and weeklies off, and the plan stops counting on what they pay.
Turn hunting on, and a sea monster drop becomes something to go and kill for, instead of something to buy. A hunt is never counted in the days.
Turn bartering off, and the barter lists stop counting. What only a barter brings drops out of the days, into the steps no rate is counted for.
What this plan will never do is written out underneath, drawn from the choices you just made, so you know what it is not counting.
Today's board waits for the layout you read on the Barter tab. Once one is read, it lists what that board deals toward this list, island by island, with a tick for each.
Then the steps, in the order they are done: the quests to run, the rewards to take, the Crow Coin Shop, bartering at sea, and so on down.
Each step says where it happens, and carries its own total.
A quest step lists each quest, how many times to run it, where it is handed in, and which reward to take from a pick-one.
Make it my pick remembers that choice, so claiming the quest on the Quests tab takes one press.
A barter step is paced by how often that offer is really on the list, from the game's own tables, not by how often it could be.
Fold a step away once you are done with it.
Every way is the other reading: everything grouped by how you get it, the Crow Coin Shop, Falasi, the Market, barter and the rest.
Each group carries a running total, measured against what is in your purse.
That is To Get. Not what you could do, but what to do, and when it ends.

== One — The Yard ==

This is the Yard: the tabs where a build is planned. Plan, Builds, Inventory, Tree and Workshop.
Start with the bar under the tabs. These are the numbers about you that every screen plans from. Click it open.
Total Barters first. Type the count your Barter window shows in game. It decides which islands will trade with you at all.
Your barter level. It sets what every exchange costs in Parley.
Tick Value Pack if one is running. It adds a draw a day and takes ten per cent off the Parley.
Corsair is for a Corsair at the wheel: one per cent more speed, acceleration, turn and brake.
Your Sailing Mastery, as the character window shows it. It counts toward speed, acceleration, turn and brake.
The sailing log you carry. Its mastery is already in your total, so only its top speed is added on top.
And your Bos'n Jacks. Their talent adds fifty LT of ship weight a tier, across the five pets you can have out. Open the nest, set all five at once, and save.
Region is where the Central Market prices are read from.
Type them once, and every tab reads them. Fold the bar away.
Now the Builds tab. This is the queue: what you mean to build, in the order you want it done.
Add a build. The picker takes ships, parts, or a stack of materials, and the chips narrow it down.
Start with a ship: the Epheria Caravel.
A Caravel can be built two ways, so it asks which. Each way shows how much material it takes in all.
Take the Improved Sailboat. It costs a little more, and adds a solo cannon volley. Either choice can be changed later.
Then a part: Chiro's Sail, for the Carrack Advance.
Each build is a card: how far along it is, and what is still to get, in coins, silver and goods.
Order matters. When stock runs short, the build at the top gets it first. Move the sail up.
The Caravel can wait. Pause it, and it stops claiming stock until you start it again.
Under the queue are the biggest blockers: the missing things holding the most up. A count can be typed right on the row.
The Plan tab is everything the active builds need, in one list.
The Next line says the one most useful thing to do right now.
Today shows your ship, the quests that pay into your list, and how long until the dailies, the weeklies and the barter refresh reset.
Then the totals: how much is covered, what is still missing, and how many recipes you can craft now.
The list is grouped. What you are building, then red for missing, blue for what is crafted from other things, and green for covered.
Filter it to the missing only, when that is all you want to see.
As you gather, type what you hold into the box. Here, a hundred Starlight Hardener.
It leaves the missing list, and every build re-plans around it at once.
Big numbers can be typed short. Two k is two thousand, and twelve thousand can be written with the separators your language uses.
Or record a whole trip in one go. Log a trip takes everything you brought back.
Pick the item and type how many. Add a line for each thing.
Record, and the whole trip goes in as one change.
So one Undo takes the whole trip back, and Redo puts it in again.
Or skip the typing. Screenshot your storage window in game, and give the shots to Read a storage on the Inventory.
Here are two shots of one storage, scrolled between. It finds the slots, names each icon it knows, and reads the count written over it. It all runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.
It says how many things it read, how many slots it passed over, and that the row both shots share was counted once.
Every line keeps the corner of its slot beside it, so a count is checked at a glance. One the reader was not sure of is marked: check this one.
Say which storage this is. Update writes what the shots show; Replace treats the shots as the whole storage.
Nothing is written until you press Write.
And there it is in the Inventory, all of it at Velia, as one change.
Trade goods are grouped by level, each with its Lv chip. Level 5 and up take a slot each; everything else takes one slot a kind.
One Undo takes the whole storage back out again.
That is the Yard: what you want built, and what you hold toward it. Next, the Workshop, where it gets made.

== Two — The Workshop ==

This is the Workshop. It makes what you have the materials for, and records the enhancement attempts you make in game.
Ready to craft lists every recipe your stock covers, and how many of each you could make right now.
The chips under each name are its ingredients: what you hold, over what one craft takes.
Type a batch size and press Craft. Here, ten Violent Wave Plywood.
Crafting moves real stock. The scales come out, and the plywood goes in.
All makes as many as the stock allows, in one press.
Where the game has a Mass Process for a recipe, tick it, and the extra it yields is counted too.
Crafted the wrong thing? Undo steps it back, and Redo puts it forward.
Enhancement is kept apart, because an attempt can fail.
Each row is a part you own that can go higher: the next level, what it adds to the ship, and the stones one attempt takes.
The chance is your real one at the failstack you type, and the line under it says what finishing the part should cost, and the most it could.
Type your failstack, and the chance moves with it.
Then record what happened in game. Failed takes the stones; a blue or green part keeps its level.
Succeeded takes the stones too, and moves the part up a level.
Yellow parts are different: a failure drops a level unless Cron Stones held it, so their rows ask which.
The Inventory is one tile per thing you hold, or still need.
Filter it to what is needed, short, owned, or free to spend.
Or by kind: materials, ship parts, trade goods, and each barter level from one to seven.
Type a name to find one thing.
Open its tile, and the panel says where it is held, which builds have reserved it, and which quests pay it.
The count can be set here too, a step at a time or typed straight in.
A count can be kept per storage. Add one, and the total is your bags plus every storage you have noted it at.
Further down, every way of getting it, priced.
The Crow Coin Shop price, against what making it costs, with its own ingredients priced all the way down.
Anything the Crow Coin Shop sells has a Buy. It asks how many, shows what is left of the purse, and records the goods and the coins as one change.
Already levelled a part in game? Find it in the Inventory.
A part and all its levels are one tile. Pick the level you reached.
Then move one to it.
The tile now holds one at plus seven. No stones were spent: this records what already happened.
The Tree shows why a build needs what it needs.
Pick a build, and its recipe opens as a tree: the part, over what it is made from, over what those are made from.
Fold any branch away, or expand them all at once.
Every line carries your count, and it can be typed in place, as on the Plan.
The map pin beside a sea material opens the Map on where it comes from.
That is the Workshop. Next, To Get: one plan for everything still missing.

== Three — To Get ==

This is To Get. Once a build is queued, it answers what is left: what to do next, and when it will be done.
Still to get, at the top: everything you are short of, biggest first, tinted by what the shop would charge for it.
Press one, and the whole screen narrows to that one thing.
Press it again, and everything is back.
The list copies out as text, or as rows for a spreadsheet.
Two readings below it. The way to get it picks one way for each thing. Every way lists them all.
The plan opens on the headline: how many days until you have the lot.
Under it, the thing that sets that pace. Barter days are always called an estimate, since which board a refresh deals is chance.
Beside it, the coins it will spend against what you can spare, and how many things are left, across how many steps.
Then it asks what matters most to you, because there is no right answer.
Soonest spends the purse wherever that buys a day.
Keep the coins spends Crow Coins only where nothing else sells the thing.
Keep the silver leaves the Central Market alone, and buys from Falasi only what only he sells.
The day count and the spend follow every choice. With this purse all three land on the same day, so the goal only moves the plan where there is a real choice to make.
Tell it how many days a week you actually sail. Three days a week, and the same plan takes longer on the calendar.
Type the Crow Coins you want to keep back. The plan never spends them, and works around what is left.
And say what you are willing to do at all.
Switch the dailies and weeklies off, and the plan stops counting on what they pay.
Turn hunting on, and a sea monster drop becomes something to go and kill for, instead of something to buy. A hunt is never counted in the days.
Turn bartering off, and the barter lists stop counting. What only a barter brings drops out of the days, into the steps no rate is counted for.
What this plan will never do is written out underneath, drawn from the choices you just made, so you know what it is not counting.
Today's board waits for the layout you read on the Barter tab. Once one is read, it lists what that board deals toward this list, island by island, with a tick for each.
Then the steps, in the order they are done: the quests to run, the rewards to take, the Crow Coin Shop, bartering at sea, and so on down.
Each step says where it happens, and carries its own total.
A quest step lists each quest, how many times to run it, where it is handed in, and which reward to take from a pick-one.
Make it my pick remembers that choice, so claiming the quest on the Quests tab takes one press.
A barter step is paced by how often that offer is really on the list, from the game's own tables, not by how often it could be.
Fold a step away once you are done with it.
Every way is the other reading: everything grouped by how you get it, the Crow Coin Shop, Falasi, the Market, barter and the rest.
Each group carries a running total, measured against what is in your purse.
That is To Get. Not what you could do, but what to do, and when it ends.

== Four — Quests ==

This is the Quests tab: the sailing dailies and weeklies, and what each one pays, set against what you are short of.
The clocks first. Dailies reset at midnight UTC, weeklies on Thursday, and the barter refresh at six in the morning.
The quests are grouped by how often they come round: daily, weekly, and once.
Each row is one quest: who gives it, where, the monster it asks for, and every reward it pays.
A quest paying something your builds need is marked, so the ones worth the detour stand out.
On the map opens the Map on where that monster is found.
Under each group, what all of them together pay in a day, or in a week.
Filter the list. Pays what I need shows only the quests paying into your builds.
Still to do hides what you have already done today.
Or pick the rewards themselves. Tick an item, and only the quests paying in it are left.
The cross on the chip takes that filter off again.
And the search finds a quest by its name, its place, or its reward.
Done one in game? Press Claimed.
Its rewards go into your stock, and the quest is ticked off until the reset.
Not done takes the tick back off. Undo takes the rewards back too.
Some quests let you pick one reward. Claimed asks which one you took.
The choice is kept, so next time Claimed records the same reward in one press.
Choose ahead answers that question now, before you have even run the quest.
Ran several? Tick each one you did.
A bar comes up with how many are ticked.
Press Finish, and every ticked quest is recorded at once. A pick-one with no choice yet asks for it on the way.
The rewards land in your stock as one change.
Got one wrong? Undo puts the whole batch back, and Redo puts it in again.
Tick all ticks every quest still open in a group. Untick all clears the ticks.
Run the same set every day? Tick them, and save them as a group under a name.
The group is a chip now. One press ticks every quest in it that is still to do.
Star the quests that matter to you, and the Favourites chip shows just those.
The one-off quests are here too, with Ravinia's log counting the letters you have recorded.
The ticks wear off at the reset by themselves, so each day starts fresh.
That is Quests. Free materials, tracked against what you are building.

== Five — Your Ship ==

This is the Ship tab. Everything the app knows about how fast you sail, and how much you carry, comes from this one screen.
At the top is the ship itself: the hull, what it is for, and its speed, its hold and how many of its five slots are fitted.
The hold is the limit in LT, and the slots it has. Past the limit the ship still sails, only slower.
Under every figure is where it comes from: the hull, the parts, the crystal, the crew and your mastery, added up.
Your Sailing Mastery is read from the bar under the tabs, where you typed it in.
Change ship lists every hull in the game, with its crew, cabin space, weight, slots and speed.
Pick the one you sail. Here, the Carrack Advance stays.
Fitted out shows the four part slots: cannon, sail, figurehead and plating.
Each slot takes the best part you hold, by itself. This Carrack has a plus ten Toro Cannon and a plus four Toro Sail.
Change opens every part that fits the slot, with what it does at plus ten, and marks the one you hold.
Pick one you do not own yet, and its level, and every figure is worked out as if it were fitted, marked as a plan.
Best I own puts back whatever you actually hold.
The fifth slot is the sea crystal. Every grade from Eltro to Rusalka is listed, with its effect.
Pick the one you have socketed, and its bonus goes into the numbers.
Then the appearance set. Here it is not only a look: a full set adds durability and rations, so tick the pieces you wear.
Now the crew. The ship is drawn with every seat it has, and the roster starts empty.
Typing a crew in, four growths each, is an evening. So Read screenshots takes the game's own Manage Sailors window instead.
Screenshot one sailor a shot, and drop them all in.
It reads them in your browser; nothing is uploaded. The window never prints a sailor's type, so it is worked out from the appetite, the cabin cost and the weight.
Check the table against the game. A type can be corrected here, and a sailor already on the roster is brought up to date instead of hired twice.
Then take them.
And there they are on the sailor list, with their real levels and growths.
Sort the list by whatever you are fitting for: best all round, or one growth at a time.
Click a sailor, and the panel on the right shows everything about them.
The growths are typed straight from the sailor window, and every figure downstream follows them.
To seat one by hand, click the sailor, then click the seat.
Where they sit matters. A sail seat doubles their speed and acceleration, and the wheel doubles turn and brake.
Or let it arrange the whole crew. Auto assign asks what the boat is for.
Each goal shows what the crew would come to under it: speed, acceleration, turn, brake, cannons, or all round.
Pick one, and it chooses who comes aboard as well as who sits where.
The row under the ship fills in. That is what the crew adds, on top of the hull and the parts.
Two presets are kept per hull, for swapping crews quickly. Save this speed crew to the first.
Arrange it for cannons, and save that to the second.
Now one click on a preset puts that crew straight back on.
A setup is bigger: the hull, its parts, the crystal and the seating, kept under a name.
Change to another hull, and its own seats and parts come up. Keep that one as a setup too.
Your fleet lists every setup you have kept, and every hull in your inventory without one.
Sail one, and it becomes the ship the Map and the Barter tab time their routes at.
And Copy link carries the whole ship, hull, parts, crystal and crew, in one address, for when someone asks what you sail.
That is your ship. Get this screen right, and every distance and every minute in the app is yours.

== Six — The Map ==

This is the Map: the whole sea, with everything you are short of marked on it.
Every pin is a barterer who can deal something on your list.
The head says how many of the ninety-one barterers that is.
Showing is what the chart marks. Everything you are short of, or one item you choose.
Pick one, and only the islands dealing that one thing are left.
Everything I am short of brings the rest back.
The pill on the chart counts down to the barter refresh, the dailies and the weeklies.
The buttons along the top are the chart's tools.
Zoom out and in. Scrolling on the sea does the same, and dragging pans.
This one fits everything marked back into view, if you get lost.
The ruler measures any two points on the sea. Click one, then the other.
It gives the distance in metres, and the time your ship takes to sail it.
This hides or shows the minimap in the corner. Click on the minimap to jump anywhere.
And 3D stands the chart up.
That is the game's own terrain, read out of the client. Not a picture of it: the real heightmap.
Shift-drag to lean it, and the islands come up out of the water.
The world curves away toward the horizon, the way the game's own map does, and the pins and the routes bend with it.
Ground paints it in the colours the client ships.
Neon draws it as contours, the way the game's own 3D map does.
And Level looks straight down again, facing north.
The last button is full screen. That is where the chart is worth using.
Now the sea has the whole window. The few buttons that matter come with it.
Fit again, now that the chart has the room.
The panel drives the chart, and the arrows swap it to the other side.
On the chart is what gets drawn, whichever tab is open.
Barterers, the habitats where each species lives, the wharf managers, guild wharves, island names, and your own drawings.
The habitats are the pictures of where each species lives. Turn them off when the chart gets busy.
The wharves mark every wharf manager, for repairs, rations and sailors.
And the island names come up once you are close enough to read them.
Five tabs: Who has it, Route, Draw, Hunt and Today. Route and Draw have a part of their own, after this one.
Who has it lists every island dealing something on your list, the most first, with what it trades for.
Narrow it to materials or to trade goods.
One of N is that island's pool: each refresh it deals one offer out of N, so this is where the thing can turn up, not where it will.
Click a row, and the chart flies to that island.
Its card lists what it trades, and adds it to a route.
Hunt is for what you kill rather than trade.
At the top, the hunting quests you have taken. Work out today's loop picks a ground for each, and puts the calls in order.
Every species is listed: sea monsters, young ones, ships, bosses, the Cox Pirates, and the Hollow Maretta, with the thirty-eight spots it rings at.
Click one, and every spawn point it has goes on the chart.
Tick as many as you like. The lot can be sent to the game's own world map as favourites.
Above them are the known sailing courses, each with a note on what it is for.
Today is a checklist for this refresh: every island you need, to tick off as you visit.
The ring counts how many you have been to, and it all clears with the barter refresh.
Escape, or the cross, brings the page back.
That is the Map. Everything you are short of, where it is, and how long it takes to get there.

== Seven — Routes and Drawings ==

The Map's Route and Draw tabs: a loop through what you are short of, timed at your own ship, and routes of your own, drawn straight onto the sea.
Route turns the list of islands into a sailing route.
Plot the loop, and it joins every island you are short of, bent round the land rather than through it.
The tiles total it up: the stops, the distance and the time at your ship's speed, the hold, the rations it eats, and the Parley it costs.
Parley is counted at one trade a stop, or at every attempt the offer allows.
Every stop is a row: what that island has for you, and how the hold stands after it.
Say where you sail from, and whether you come back, and the times follow.
Click a stop, and the chart flies to it.
The cross takes a stop off, and a pin's card puts one back on.
The route is timed at the ship you sail. Switch setup times it at another one you have kept.
Reverse sails it the other way round.
Save keeps it by name. Eight are kept, and none is dropped without asking.
Copy link puts the route in an address. Signed in, it is a short one.
Export and Import carry it as a small file instead.
And Put it on the game's map writes the stops into Black Desert's own world map.
As favourites, named and numbered, or as one of the map's three navigation loops.
Copy the block, or download it, and paste it into gameVariable.xml at the character screen. Or let the app write it into the game's folder itself, keeping a copy of the old file.
Draw is for routes the list cannot describe.
Four tools: add stops, draw, write, and shade an area.
Add stops drops a numbered stop wherever you click, in the order you mean to sail. Legs bend round the land.
Pick another ink, and the next stops start a second line, numbered on its own.
Draw is a freehand line.
Write puts a note straight onto the water.
And shade an area marks a stretch of sea: click its corners, then the first one again.
Undo takes back the last mark, whatever kind it was.
Give it a name and a note.
Keep puts it on the shelf below.
Copy link carries the whole drawing, stops, lines, words and all, to anyone, with nothing of theirs touched.
To the game writes its lines into the game's map too, a loop each.
The shelf keeps the newest; the eye lays one over the chart beside what you are drawing.
And Browse opens the whole library, with a search and every drawing as a card.
That is routing and drawing. A loop timed at your own ship, and a way to show anyone else the sea you mean.

== Eight — Today’s Board ==

Bartering, part one. Before the app can plan a run, it has to know which board the game dealt you.
The Barter tab starts with what today is for. Silver, a stock and Crow Coins all sail the trade goods list. A material reads the ship material list.
A Barter Refresh deals one of those two lists, never both. This part is about the trade goods list; materials have a part of their own.
The trade goods list is always one of the game’s forty layouts. Name the layout, and every island’s offer is known.
There are three ways to name it: one island, a screenshot, or another sailor’s reading.
This button is the quickest by hand. It asks about the island that tells the layouts apart best — here, Baeza Island.
But you can look at any island. Click "another island".
The islands that split the layouts best come first. Islands your Total Barters have not opened are greyed, with the count that opens them.
Type Eveto, and pick it.
Now it lists every offer Eveto can show, and how many of the forty layouts have each one.
In the game, open Barter Information and find Eveto Island. It hands over Essence of Liquor for a Level 1 Cherry Tree Seed Pouch.
Pick that same trade here.
One island was enough. Today is layout 4.
Every chain this board allows is already worked out further down the tab.
Beside the layout: your barter count and barter level, taken from the sailor bar at the top of the page.
And your Parley. A dashed outline means assumed: the bar refills to one million at every Barter Refresh, so a full bar is the guess until you type yours.
The islands you looked at are kept here, with an Undo for the last one.
A layout does not fix every island. This chip says how many islands draw their offer at random.
Some Level 4 islands pay a Level 5 good on one refresh and Crow Coins on another. Until you say which, the likelier one is planned on.
Pujara Island shows Crow Coins in my window, so I click that.
Further down, islands that draw from a pool of offers: materials, Tidal Black Stone, a Lost Trade Box. Nothing is planned on those until you say what they show.
This line counts the islands your barter count has not opened. The game opens each exchange at its own count, so these show nothing to you today.
"Which ones" lists them, grouped by the count that opens them. The run is planned without them.
Your count lives in the sailor bar. Open it, and set Total Barters to 1,082 — a newer sailor.
Now many more islands are shut, and every chain that climbs through one is gone from the plan.
Put it back to 4,205. Recording a trip adds its trades to this count for you.
Now the second way. Press "Refreshed in game" — what you do after every refresh — and the board is a question again.
Take a screenshot of the same Barter Information window, and paste it with Control V — or click the box and choose the file.
Every row is read: the island, what it takes, and what it pays.
Each row is matched against what that island is known to deal, so a name the window cut short still reads. Every row can be corrected from its list.
Answer the six islands, and the layout is named from all of them at once. Nothing is uploaded; the reading happens in your browser.
The third way needs other sailors. The fleet in this part is staged: a machine recording a film has nobody else on its server.
Throw the board away once more.
When a signed-in sailor has read today’s board, the bar says so: who read it, how many islands, and how many others saw the same.
Click "take their reading", and every island they named is answered for you.
The layout book keeps all forty layouts.
Each card shows the land goods its Level 1 islands want, the goods its coin islands take, how many exchanges it has at each level, and how often it has been seen.
Filter them: all forty, the ones still standing today, the ones the fleet has read, and yours — the boards you have been dealt yourself.
Above the shelf: boards that sailors read and that fit no layout on file, each with the layout it is nearest to.
Open a layout, and it lists every island level by level. Islands you saw today are ticked; the ones above your count are locked.
The level chips narrow it, say to the Level 5 exchanges.
And the search finds a layout by an island, a good or its number.
Sometimes an island in game shows something the layout does not. Click "An island shows something else".
It lists every island, and beside each one what today’s board says it shows. Pick the island.
Ostra Island should show Rare Herb Pile. Mine shows Gooey Monster Blood, so I choose "Something else"…
…and pick it from every exchange Ostra is known to deal.
The board is now layout 4 with one island as you saw it. The game moves a slot at a maintenance without renumbering the layout, and the run is planned on what you saw.
That is a reading worth passing on, so "Tell the fleet" appears. It needs you signed in: the reading goes up with your name on it.
One last press of "Refreshed in game". Besides forgetting the board, it sets your Parley back to a full bar, because the game refills it at every refresh.
Choose a material, and the tab reads the ship material list instead. Naming one list puts down the other, because a refresh deals only one.
That is the board. The next parts plan runs on it.

== Nine — A Silver Day ==

Bartering, part two. A day for silver: climb the chains on today’s board and sell the tops at a wharf.
The board is already named — layout 4, as in part one. This sailor has last week’s goods in the Iliya storage, at every level.
One note: the Central Market in this film is a recorded day, so the run plans the same every time it is shot.
The plan has four parts, and each one opens when the one before is settled.
One: Before you sail.
The Parley in your bar now, as the head of the barter window shows it. This sailor has already spent some today: 742,300 is left.
Vouchers carried. Each Crow’s Trade Voucher puts 250,000 Parley back, on a two-hour cooldown.
Your home port: where the run leaves from, comes back to, and whose storage it loads from. This sailor keeps goods at Iliya Island, so click Iliya.
How long a stop takes apart from the sailing. Only the clock uses these.
And your bag as a second hold, which has a part of its own.
Two: where today ends.
This ladder runs from the shore to Level 7. Under each level: how many you hold, what happens to them, and how many to keep back.
Click a level, and the climb ends there. At Level 5, nothing is carried past it.
Click Level 7 again. Today we climb to the top.
The tag under a level says what the wharf does with it. Tap "sold" and Level 5 is kept as stock instead…
…tap again, and it is sold.
A keep number is a floor. Keep 4 at Level 4, and the run never hands over or sells the last four.
The line under the ladder says it in words: climb to Level 7, sell Level 5 and up, and how many chains on today’s board fit that.
Three: how to sail it. Five ways, and under each, the best run the search finds sailing that way today.
Light and fast keeps the hold under its limit, and calls at a wharf only when one lies on the way and pays for the minute it costs.
An hour at most is the best run that fits in about an hour. Click a card, and the run is sailed that way; the line under each card says what your own ticked chains come to sailed that way.
The whole board at full speed takes every trade, and drops goods at a wharf whenever the hold fills.
Loaded heavy takes every trade too: the wharf loads to the limit, the exchanges push the hold past it, and the ship sails slower with fewer wharf calls.
Spend no silver uses only goods you already own.
My own way opens every order behind those cards.
Where land goods come from — bought ashore, from your storage, or only what is held. The pace. A time limit. Whether vouchers are drawn on. The way round. Which storage the wharf calls use. BreezySail, for the rations. And quests on the way.
Also trade for: chains that end in ship materials, Great Ocean goods, and Lost Trade Boxes. Great Ocean goods are on by default and sold like a Level 7.
A way of sailing you like can be kept under a name. Click "save these"…
…name it, and it is one click next time: the ladder, the orders, the port, all set back at once.
Back to Light and fast.
Four: the chains on offer.
It has already found the runs worth sailing on this board: the most silver, the most an hour, and the most for each unit of Parley. They are rarely the same run.
Click one, and its chains are ticked for you.
Each card is one chain. Where its first good comes from, the levels it climbs, its islands in order, and what it pays sailed on its own: silver, silver per Parley unit, silver an hour.
Start from: a chain can start at the shore, or from a good you already hold part-way up the same climb. Here, the Round Knives in the Iliya storage.
This chain starts from Level 4 Panaceas in the Iliya storage, loaded before casting off. Nothing is bought for it.
A chain that starts ashore buys its first good on the Central Market, and is held to what is listed there. This one needs Fine Fancy Feathers, and the Market has none, so it cannot be ticked and says why.
Search the chains for an island or a good, or narrow them to what is held, what is bought ashore, or the level a chain reaches.
Or tick chains yourself. Every tick re-plans the whole run.
"Fill the rest for me" adds the best chains around the ones you picked.
Under the chains, the run adds up.

== Eleven — Under Sail ==

Bartering, part four. Under sail: one stop at a time, and the trip recorded at the end.
Light and fast from Iliya, on a bar just refilled, with everything the run needs ticked aboard.
Before you cast off, the clock’s chimes. A ship’s bell by default — click it to hear the next sound: three beeps, or none.
"Notify me too" asks the browser for a notification as well, so a chime lands while the game has the screen. Signed in, a chip beside it sends the chimes to every device on your account.
Click "Cast off".
The ship sets sail. The clock starts at this run’s own estimate, and the tab moves to the third step: one stop at a time.
This bar is the clock: the time to the next stop, and which stop it is.
It chimes at each stop — a pair of bells as each comes up, and eight bells at the end — or only once, for the whole run.
"Again" starts it over from now; "stop" puts it away.
The stop, in letters you can read across the room: the island, what you hand over, what you get back, and how many times.
Under it: the hold before and after, the Parley, and the rations.

The hold, as it stands now and after this stop, with its slots.

Arrived.

Back to the Barter tab.

== Twelve — A Stock Day ==

The ladder now says where the stock ends: Level 4, by default. Level 5 and up are not sailed.
Under each level, a target: how many to keep of every good at that level. Ten of each Level 1, thirty of each Level 2 and 3, forty of each Level 4.
Beside it, how far short the storage is. Every level here is short.
A target is a floor as well. The run fills a level before anything climbs from it, and never spends below it.
And what the day is for: the stock itself, or the barter count — every trade toward the next unlock, whatever it trades.
Down to the chains.
This note says why every chain stops low: four levels are being filled, not climbed off. Nothing climbs from them until the stock is made up.

Take the fullest stock.
Each chain says where it stops, and why: the good it makes is still short of its target, so it stays as stock.
The run: what the stock gains, level by level.
The trades, with your barter count before and after.

== Sixteen — The Character Bag ==

Bartering, part nine. Your character’s bag, used as a second hold.
Same sailor, same board, sailing from Iliya. Your inventory weighs nothing on the ship, so goods for a later chain can ride in it.
It is switched on under Before you sail. Find "your bag", and click "a second hold".
It needs the two bars at the foot of the game’s Inventory window. Weight: 612.4 of a 2,150 LT limit.
And Inventory Slot: 96 of 192 filled.
Or click "Read the Inventory window" and give it a screenshot: it reads both.
The app works out the room: a bag carries up to 170 percent of your weight limit, less what it already holds, in the slots still empty. Here, 3,042 LT and 96 slots.
Goods in the bag go in and out at any wharf’s Load Cargo. Sales stay at the seven wharves with a storage keeper. And Level 5 and up, and Great Ocean goods, take a slot each in the bag too.
Now sail it "An hour at most".

== Seventeen — The Harbour ==

Everything so far runs in your browser alone. This part has other sailors in it, and it needs sign-in.
One note first: the sailors on these boards are invented. A machine recording this has no server with real players on it. The tab, the digest and the ranking are the app's own.
Signing in with Discord puts you on the boards by name. The first time, the tab says so, and what is shared: the numbers, never your stock, your notes or your traces.
Change how you are shown picks your Discord name, or an unnamed sailor: still counted and ranked, but shown only as a sailor.
Under it is the exact digest that would be shared right now. Nothing else of your save goes up.
Your own places sit at the top, so you can see where you stand without hunting for yourself.
Sixteen boards, in five sections: the sea, the runs, quests and hunts, the yard, and charts and hold.
Press a section to see just its boards.
The question mark on a board says exactly how it is counted.
And Find a sailor searches the boards by name. An unnamed sailor cannot be found that way; that is what unnamed means.
Click any place on a board to open that sailor's card.
Their fleet, their crew, their places, and what they have done at sea.
Look stands their ship up on your own Ship tab, fully fitted and crewed.
To look at, not to keep. One press puts yours back.
The other half of the tab adds the whole fleet up.
Which hulls people sail, which parts they fit, which crystals, which islands they plot routes through, and which quests they run.
Click a line, and its card opens: what it costs, and what it is made from.
Search it for anything, and sort by most first or A to Z.
Leave the boards takes your places off, and deletes the digest the server holds.
Your save is untouched, and signing in again will not quietly put you back. Stay keeps you on.
One more door, in the Menu: Feedback.
Something wrong, an idea, or something else. It reaches whoever runs this copy of the app.
A report is a post, not a form: bold, lists, quotes, a link, and a screenshot pasted straight in.
Preview shows it the way it will be read.
Your name, the screen you were on and the build you are running go with it, so nobody has to ask which version.
The Menu holds the rest of the app: Find, the trip log, Undo and Redo, at the top.
Profiles keep separate saves on one browser. Export and Import move a save as a file or a link.
Help plays this film, the Tour walks through your own screen, and What's new lists each release.
And the theme, the water behind the page, and the language, which changes the app and every item name together.
Your data stays in your browser. Sign in to sync it between machines, or to join in. Neither is required for anything else in the app.

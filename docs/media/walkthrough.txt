== One — The Yard ==

This is the Yard. It works out what your builds need, and what you still have to find.
But start with the bar under the tabs. These are the numbers the whole app plans from.
Your total barter count matters most. It decides which islands will even deal with you.
Your barter level, which sets what every exchange costs in Parley.
Your Sailing Mastery, which counts toward how fast your ship sails.
Your region, because every silver price in the app is quoted in it.
And the Bos'n Jacks you have out. It is the one pet talent in the game that is ship weight — fifty LT a tier, across the five the game lets out at once — so a nest of them is hundreds of LT the hold was being told it did not have.
Type them once here and every screen reads them. Then fold it away.
Now queue something to build.
Some ships can be built two ways, so it asks which.
It shows what each route costs. Either is fine.
Queue a second one. Order matters: if stock runs short, the build at the top gets it first.
The Plan is everything your queue needs, in one list.
Red is missing. Blue you can make. Green you already have.
As you gather, type in what you have.
Every build re-plans around it straight away.
Or log a whole trip at once, instead of a box at a time.
The Workshop makes anything you have the materials for.
Crafting moves real stock. Ingredients out, the product in.
Made a mistake? Undo steps it back, and Redo puts it forward.
Enhancing is kept separate, because attempts can fail.
It shows your real odds at your failstack, and the most the part can end up costing.
The Inventory is one tile per thing you own, or still need.
Open one, and every way of getting it is priced.
What the shop charges, against what making it costs — including its own ingredients, all the way down.
Already levelled a part in game? Record it here.
Tell it the level you reached. It spends no stones to agree with you.
The Tree shows why a build needs what it needs.
And To Get turns all of that into a plan — which is the whole of the next part.
That is the Yard. What you want, what it takes, and what is left to find.

== Two — To Get ==

This is To Get. It answers the only question that matters once a build is queued: what do I actually do next.
Up here is what you are still short of, and what the shop would want for the part of it that can be bought.
Two readings. "Every way" lists each thing and every source it has. "The way to get it" picks one.
And the plan is the one it opens on.
This is the headline: how many days until you have the lot.
What it will cost in coins, and how many things are left across how many steps.
It asks what matters most to you, because there is no right answer.
Soonest spends the purse wherever that buys a day.
Keep the coins spends them only where nothing else sells the thing.
Keep the silver leaves the Central Market alone.
Watch the day count as you choose. That is the trade, in the only unit that matters.
Tell it how many days a week you actually sail, and the estimate follows.
Hold coins back, and it plans around what is left.
And say what you are willing to do at all.
Turn hunting on and a sea monster drop becomes something to go and kill for, instead of something to buy.
Turn bartering off and the lists stop counting. The plan gets longer and says so.
Below that are the steps themselves, in order, each one saying where it comes from and how long it takes.
A barter step is paced by how often that offer was really on the list, not by how often it could be.
And it tells you what it will never do, so you know what it is not counting.
If you would rather see the whole spread, Every way groups it by source instead.
Either reading copies out, as a list or as a spreadsheet.
That is To Get. Not what you could do, but what to do, and when it ends.

== Three — Quests ==

This is the Quests tab. It tracks the quests that pay in things you are already short of.
These are the sailing dailies and weeklies, all in one list.
The ones paying something on your build list are marked, so you can see which are worth the detour.
Filter them by what they pay, or by who gives them.
Each row shows what it pays, and how much of it you still need.
Some quests let you pick your reward. Those ask you which when you finish.
Tick off the ones you did in game.
Then click Finish.
The rewards go straight into your stock, as one change.
Got one wrong? Undo puts the whole batch back.
The ticks clear themselves at the daily reset, so you start each day fresh.
And these clocks tell you how long you have: dailies, weeklies, and the barter refresh.
Star the ones you always do, and they sort to the top.
Or save a set you run together, and tick the whole group at once.
Every quest knows where its giver stands, so a run can hand them in as it passes.
That is Quests. Free materials, tracked against what you are building.

== Four — Your Ship ==

This is the Ship tab. Everything the app knows about how fast you sail comes from this one screen.
Start with the hull. Pick the ship you actually sail.
A ship has four part slots: figurehead, plating, cannon and sail.
Each one fills itself with the best part you own.
Click Change to pick a different one.
It lists every part that fits the slot, with what each does, and marks the ones you actually hold.
The fifth slot is your sea crystal.
Pick the one you have socketed, and its bonus goes into the numbers.
Below that is the appearance set.
Skins are cosmetic in most games. Here they are not: a full set adds real durability and rations, so tick the pieces you are wearing.
And this panel at the top is the ship itself: its speed, its hold limit, and how many of the five slots are filled.
The small print under each figure says where it came from — hull, parts, crystal, crew and mastery, added up.
Your Sailing Mastery goes in here, and those figures move with it.
Now the crew. This roster starts empty.
Typing eighteen sailors in, four growths each, is an evening you will not enjoy. So it reads them off your screenshots instead.
Screenshot the Manage Sailors window in game, one sailor a shot, and drop them in. It reads them in your browser — nothing is uploaded.
Check the table against the game, then take the ones that are right.
And there they are, hired, with their real levels and growths.
Sort the list by whatever you are fitting for. Best all round, best for speed, and so on.
To seat one by hand, click the sailor, then click the seat you want them in.
Where they sit matters. A sail seat doubles their speed growth, and the wheel doubles turn and brake.
Or let it arrange the whole crew for you. Click Auto assign.
It asks what the boat is for, and shows what each answer would actually give you.
Pick one, and it seats everybody.
And the row under the ship fills in. That is what the crew is adding, on top of the hull.
Two hot presets, for swapping crews quickly. Save the current one to a slot.
And click it to put that crew straight back on.
A setup is bigger: the whole fit-out, hull, parts, crystal and seating, under a name.
The fleet lists every setup you have kept, and every hull you own without one.
The one you are sailing is what the Map times its routes at.
And you can copy the whole thing as a link, if someone asks what you sail.
That is your ship. Get this screen right, and every distance and every minute in the app is yours.

== Five — The Map ==

This is the Map. It is the whole sea, with everything you need marked on it.
Every pin is a barterer holding something you are short of.
This says what it is marking. Everything you are short of, or one item you choose.
These buttons across the top are the chart controls.
Zoom out, and zoom in. You can also just scroll on the sea.
This one fits everything marked back into view, if you get lost.
This toggles the minimap in the corner.
And this one stands the chart up.
That is the game's own terrain, read out of your client. Not a picture of it — the real heightmap.
Shift-drag to lean, and the islands come up out of the water.
And the world curves away towards the horizon, the way the game's own map does. The pins, the route and your drawings all bend with it.
Ground paints it in the colours the client ships.
Neon draws it as contours, the way the game's own world map does.
And Level looks straight down again, facing north.
And this is full screen. That is where the chart is worth using, so let us go there.
Now the sea has the whole window.
Hit fit again, now that the chart has the room. That pulls back until every mark is in view at once.
Which is most of the sea, so zoom back in a couple of steps to where you actually work.
The panel on the left is what drives it.
Start with "On the chart". This controls what gets drawn.
Barterers, habitats, wharves, guild wharves, island names, and your own drawings. Turn off what you do not need.
There is a search, for finding an island or a good by name.
And these five tabs are the tools. We will take them in order.
Barter is the default. Every island that has something on your list, with what it trades.
Click a row and the chart flies to that island.
Route turns that list into a sailing route.
Plot the loop, and it draws one, bent around the land rather than through it.
Every leg gets a distance and a time, at the speed your ship actually sails.
Save the route, share it as a link, or send it to the game map as bookmarks.
Draw is for routes the list cannot describe. It gives you three tools.
The pin drops a numbered stop, in the order you mean to sail.
The pen draws a freehand line.
And the text tool types a note straight onto the water.
Name it and keep it, and it joins your library. Traces share as links too.
Grounds is for hunting.
It lists every species: sea monsters, young ones, ships, bosses, the Cox Pirates — and the Hollow Maretta, the song the Great Ocean is told to sail away from, with the thirty-eight spots it is rung at.
Click one, and every spawn point it has goes on the chart.
Tick as many as you like, and send the lot to the game map.
Above them are the known sailing courses, each with a note.
Today is your checklist for this refresh.
Every island you need, ticked off as you visit it, with the total at the top.
Drag to pan, scroll to zoom. The drawings and pins move with the chart.
Escape, or the cross, brings the page back.
That is the Map. Everything you are short of, where it is, and how long it takes to get there.

== Six — A Run ==

This is the Barter tab. Show it one island, and it plans a whole run for you.
Next, tell it what the board looks like.
Every refresh, the sea uses one of forty layouts.
The app cannot know which one. So you show it a single island.
Click "another island", and pick the one you are looking at.
Now check that island in game. Open Barter Information, set the filter to Normal to Level 1, and find its row. Eveto Island is offering Essence of Liquor, for a Level 1 Cherry Tree Seed Pouch.
Pick that same trade here.
That names the layout. Every chain this refresh allows is now listed below.
First, what the day is for. There are four of them, and they are not the same run.
Silver climbs the chains on the board and sells the tops at a wharf.
A stock sails the same board to fill the storage instead. Nothing is sold at all.
Say how many of every good you want at a level. That is a floor as well as a target, so a level fills before anything climbs from it.
And climb no higher than stops the ladder where your stock ends — a Level 4 you already hold is stock, not fuel.
So the run is scored on what it banks: the goods it is short of, the runs left, and the storage slots they will take.
Crow Coins is the third. Every board has ten to fourteen islands paying in coins, and they take a Level 4 and nothing else.
So a coin day is a climb to four, cashed in — counted in coins, against what your builds are still short of.
And a material climbs the ladder to one thing on your list.
We will sail this one for silver.
The barter count and level you set at the start are doing real work here: the count decides which islands are on the board at all, and the level prices every Parley figure below.
Now tell it what you want out of the run.
Sell the top, keep a floor finishes every island and sells the top, but leaves something of every level for tomorrow's board.
These boxes are that floor. It is what the selling never touches — which on an empty storage means there is nothing left to sell, and the tab says so rather than proposing a run.
Cash out today is the other one: the most silver at the wharf tonight, from what is aboard and at the harbour. That is the day we will sail.
Pace. Fast keeps you light and quick.
Full and loaded fills the hold past the limit. More goods, slower sailing.
Quests on the way hands in dailies you have already taken, as you pass them.
Trade vouchers are yours to spend or keep. Drawn on, one goes in as soon as the run needs it and a whole quarter fits, which starts its two-hour cooldown as early as it can be started.
The way round sets the route order: nearest island first, or one chain at a time.
And here: where you sail from, where your storage is, and a time limit if you want one.
A way of running you like can be kept under a name — the orders, the targets and the ceiling with them.
Now the chains. It has already worked out three runs for you.
The most silver, the most per hour, and the most per Parley. They are rarely the same run.
Click one to use it.
Or tick chains yourself.
Fill the rest adds the best chains around the ones you picked.
The bar at the bottom totals it up: chains, silver, hours, islands.
Click "Lay it out".
The sheet opens on two shelves, tiled the way the game's own window is.
Load before casting off is what to take with you, and what it costs to buy ashore.
In the storage after is what comes home — and what was sold on the way.
Below them, every stop in order. What you hand over, and what you get back.
This bar is your hold after each trade.
Orange means you are over the limit. You still sail, just slower.
Red means too heavy to barter. You have to lighten first.
That is what a wharf stop is for. Drop the extra into storage, and carry on.
And this bar is your Parley going down as you trade.
Before you cast off, the clock at the foot of the sheet.
It starts at this run's own estimate and counts up, and it carries your own pace: seconds for bartering and going on, seconds for a wharf or a quest.
It rings a ship's bell at every stop rather than only at the end — a pair struck as each stop comes up, eight bells when the run is done.
And it reaches every device signed in to your account, so the phone in your pocket rings with this tab shut.
Start it.
Happy with it? Click "Sail this run".
It goes onto the map as a checklist.
Full screen, if you want more sea and less panel.
It follows you leg by leg, and every stop is a card with the trade on it.
Tick each stop as you make it. If an island pays a different amount, everything after it re-counts.
Out of the sheet it keeps time on the tab itself. Ticking a stop off re-bases the rest, so a slow island does not make the whole run chime early.
Finished sailing? Tick them all off at once.
Then click "Record the trip".
Silver goes to your purse, leftover goods to your inventory, and the quests are claimed.
All as one change, so one undo puts it back.
And the trip joins today's boards.
Every run since the refill: what it loaded, what it came back with, and the day's totals across the Parley bar.
What it will not do is guess the next board. A refresh deals a different one, and the panel says so.
One last thing. The hold size, the speed and every time on that route come from your ship.
Hull, the four parts, the crystal and the crew aboard. Change the ship, and the whole run re-times.
That is it. Show it one island, and it plans the rest.

== Seven — The Harbour ==

Everything so far runs in your browser alone. This last part is the one that needs sign-in.
One note first: the sailors on these boards are invented. A machine recording this has no server with real players on it. The tab, the digest and the ranking are the app's own.
Sixteen boards, built from the players who chose to join them.
They come in five sections: the sea, the runs, quests and hunts, the yard, and charts and hold.
The question mark on a board tells you exactly how that one is counted.
Your own places sit at the top, so you can see where you stand without hunting for yourself.
And there is a search, if you are after one sailor by name.
Click any place on a board to open that player's card.
Their fleet, their crew, their places, and what they have done at sea.
Click Look, and their ship loads onto your own Ship tab, fully fitted.
To look at, not to keep. One click puts yours back.
The other half of the tab adds the whole fleet up.
Which hulls people sail, which parts they fit, which crystals, which quests they run.
Search it for anything, and sort by most-first or A to Z.
It is the closest thing to an answer when you are wondering what everyone else fits.
Nothing goes onto a board that you did not offer.
You see the exact digest before you agree to it — the numbers, and nothing else.
And leaving takes it back off again.
One more door, under Menu. Feedback.
Something wrong, an idea, or something else — and it reaches whoever runs this copy of the app.
A report is a post, not a form. Bold, lists, quotes, a link — and a screenshot pasted straight in.
Preview shows it the way it will be read.
Your name, the screen you were on and the build you are running go with it, so nobody has to ask which version.
It is worth doing. Five players wrote in before this release, and one of them wrote most of it.
Your data stays in your browser. Sign in to sync it between machines, or to join in. Neither is required for anything else in the app.
